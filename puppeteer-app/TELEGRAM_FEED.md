# Compact subscriber feed

The user chose a maximum of **20 signals per WAT day**, at least **30 minutes apart**. This is a ceiling, not a target. Send one compact, qualified upcoming pick per card; stay quiet when no engine qualifies. No raw fixture lists, parallel confidence streams, repeated results, streak celebrations or automatic recap posts are part of the compact feed. Old subscriber messages and logs are preserved.

The previous configuration enabled both all-fixture and confidence streams. The result condition also treated confidence-picks enabled as permission to send results, even when the results switch was off. The read-only audit found80 logged cards/hour (four streams of20), about1,900/day. Repeated worker calls also checked deduplication before network I/O and recorded it only afterward, creating a race. Automatic retries after a timeout could repeat a message whose delivery had actually succeeded.

Compact policy is loaded from server-owned telegramFeedStyle, telegramDailySignalLimit and telegramSignalGapMinutes settings. Stale browser configuration cannot reopen raw streams. Manual force requests still use the same qualification, deduplication, daily limit and spacing. The Telegram Hub shows the cap and spacing; direct confidence bulk sending is blocked in compact mode. Existing official publication/model/version/nighttime guards remain required. Experimental ML observations do not enter the subscriber feed. Only real captured odds and pending fixtures60seconds to15minutes ahead are candidates.

telegram_curated_feed.js uses a shared databases/telegram_delivery.db across producer processes and source databases. An immediate SQLite transaction reserves the canonical bot/provider/round/kickoff event and its distinct recipients before network I/O. It enforces a bot-wide daily cap and cooldown, including attempts with uncertain outcomes. Different engines or message formats cannot resend that round. A restart or clearing the legacy dedup log does not clear this ledger. VIP-only routing respects the target selection; overlapping group/subscriber destinations are deduplicated.

telegram_transport.js retries only explicit429 rate-limit rejections, at most three attempts. Network errors, timeouts and malformed responses are uncertain and are not automatically resent. Ledger state records sent, failed or uncertain delivery per recipient; successful recipients are never resent because a different recipient failed. An interrupted in-flight reservation also remains held for review. This favors avoiding duplicates over silently retrying an uncertain alert; ambiguous delivery is not proof of success.

Saved Telegram message IDs let an exact settled source pick update its original card through [editMessageText](https://core.telegram.org/bots/api#editmessagetext). Both wins and losses are displayed. No new result message is sent. Missing scores, a reused fixture ID or mismatched fixture identity cannot edit another card. Concurrent workers claim edits; failed edits have a bounded retry policy and never fall back to sending another message. Existing pre-migration spam cards have no saved delivery IDs and are not replayed, deleted or rewritten.

All verification uses fake transports and temporary databases; never send subscriber test messages. Qualification waits, archives and model training remain unchanged.
